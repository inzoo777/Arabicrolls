1. The 3 Parts of Speech أَقْسَامُ الْكَلَامِ

Every word in the Arabic language falls into one of three distinct categories:

1. Noun (Ism) اِسْم

Includes names of people, places, objects, adjectives, adverbs, and pronouns. Nouns do not carry time/tense.

مَدْرَسَة
Madrasah
School

2. Verb (Fi'l) فِعْل

Words indicating an action bound to a time frame (Past مَاضٍ, Present/Future مُضَارِع, or Imperative أَمْر).

يَكْتُبُ
Yaktubu
He writes / is writing

3. Particle (Harf) حَرْف

Connecting words that carry no complete meaning on their own unless attached to nouns or verbs.

إِلَى
Ilā
To / Towards

2. Demonstrative Pronouns أَسْمَاءُ الْإِشَارَةِ

Words used to point at specific objects or people based on distance and gender:

Distance Gender Arabic Transliteration English Equivalent
Near Masculine هَذَا Hādhā This (Male/Inanimate M)
Near Feminine هَذِهِ Hādhihi This (Female/Inanimate F)
Far Masculine ذَلِكَ Dhālika That (Male/Inanimate M)
Far Feminine تِلْكَ Tilka That (Female/Inanimate F)

3. Detached Personal Pronouns الضَّمَائِرُ المُنْفَصِلَةُ

Pronouns that stand independently in a sentence:

Person Arabic Transliteration English Meaning
1st Person (Singular) أَنَا Ana I
1st Person (Plural) نَحْنُ Naḥnu We
2nd Person (Male) أَنْتَ Anta You (Singular Male)
2nd Person (Female) أَنْتِ Anti You (Singular Female)
3rd Person (Male) هُوَ Huwa He / It
3rd Person (Female) هِيَ Hiya She / It

4. Sentence Structure أَنْوَاعُ الْجُمَلِ

Arabic sentences are classified by the category of the opening word:

Nominal Sentence جُمْلَة اسْمِيَّة

Starts with a Noun. Composed of a Subject (مُبْتَدَأ) and Predicate (خَبَر).

الـشَّمْسُ طَالِعَةٌ
Ash-shamsu ṭāli'ah
The sun is rising

Verbal Sentence جُمْلَة فِعْلِيَّة

Starts with a Verb. Composed of Verb (فِعْل) and Doer/Subject (فَاعِل).

نَامَ الطِّفْلُ
Nāma aṭ-ṭiflu
The child slept

5. Common Prepositions حُرُوفُ الْجَرِّ

Prepositions always precede a noun and cause the noun to enter the Genitive state (Majroor):

Arabic Transliteration Meaning Example
فِي Fī In / Inside فِي الْبَيْتِ (In the house)
عَلَى 'Alā On / Upon عَلَى الطَّاوِلَةِ (On the table)
مِنْ Min From مِنَ الْمَدِينَةِ (From the city)
إِلَى Ilā To / Towards إِلَى السُّوقِ (To the market)

6. Sun & Moon Letters الحُرُوفُ الشَّمْسِيَّةُ وَالقَمَرِيَّةُ

When adding the definite article AL (الـ) to a noun, pronunciation changes based on the first letter:

Sun Letters الحُرُوف الشَّمْسِيَّة

The "L" sound in AL is assimilated into the letter. (14 Letters: ت, ث, د, ذ, ر, ز, س, ش, ص, ض, ط, ظ, ل, ن).

الشَّمْسُ
Ash-Shams
(L is silent)

Moon Letters الحُرُوف القَمَرِيَّة

The "L" sound in AL is clearly pronounced. (14 Letters: أ, ب, ج, ح, خ, ع, غ, ف, ق, ك, م, هـ, و, ي).

الْقَمَرُ
Al-Qamar
(L is clearly voiced)
